# Review: before-sapiens

## Verdict
**Accept with major revisions**, leaning borderline. The scholarship is unusually clean — the references are real and accurately cited, the prose is genuinely good, and the author is honest that the "simulation" measures nothing new. But the paper's one quantitative novelty, the §10 "stratigraphy measured," is a category-mixing arithmetic exercise whose headline result ("seven of eight … all eight") rests on a single contested date that is (a) attributed to a source giving a *different* number and (b) contradicted by the most recent literature. The single most serious problem: the wooden-weapons date of 300,000 years is cited to Thieme 1997, which actually reported **~400,000**, while a 2025 re-dating puts the Schöningen Spear Horizon at **~200,000** — and the paper's "all eight precede Omo I" claim collapses if the newest date is right.

## Claim grounding
- *"seven of eight … predate the boundary … Measured against the Omo I minimum of 233,000 years, all eight do."* (§10) → **Overstated / fragile.** Internally consistent with the sim's chosen inputs, but the result is hostage to one number. At Thieme's own 400 ka it would be "eight of eight"; at the 2025 value (~200 ka) wooden weapons clears *neither* boundary and the claim becomes "six of eight / seven of eight." The cleanest-story value (0.3) is the one used.
- *"its median member precedes the boundary by 1.885 million years"* (§10) → **Arithmetically correct, substantively hollow.** I reproduced 1.885 from the code. But it is the median of first-appearance dates of ontologically different things (an anatomy, four lithic horizons, a dispersal event, a fire trace). A median over that set is not a meaningful quantity.
- *Abstract enumeration* — "bipedal locomotion, stone-flake production, long-range dispersal, large-animal processing, Acheulean biface manufacture, fire use, wooden weapon production, and **extended social learning**" → **Inconsistent with the measured set.** This list of eight includes "extended social learning" (no defensible date — §10 explicitly excludes such) and omits "Oldowan technology," which *is* in the measured eight. A reader counting "seven of eight" against the abstract's own list counts a different eight.
- *"large-animal butchery … 2.90 Ma … (3.0–2.6 Ma; midpoint used)"* (sim) → **Stated method ≠ value.** The midpoint of 3.0 and 2.6 is **2.8**, not 2.9. Doesn't change any predates/median outcome, but the annotation is wrong.
- *"Green and colleagues showed that Neanderthals contributed … a few percent of the genome in living people outside Africa"* (§7) → **Supported.** Green 2010 estimated 1–4%.
- *"Sima de los Huesos … genetically closer to Neanderthals than to Denisovans"* (§7) → **Supported** (Meyer 2016 nuclear DNA; correctly avoids the 2014 mtDNA-Denisovan red herring).
- *"Stone-flake production at Lomekwi precedes the earliest secure Homo"* (§3, §10) → **Supported** (3.3 Ma vs ~2.8 Ma Ledi-Geraru).
- *"Homo floresiensis survives on Flores until roughly 50,000 years ago"* (§7) → **Supported** (Sutikna 2016 revision).

## Argument
- **§10 (the contribution) is close to tautological.** Selecting capacities by "single securely dated first appearance" and then observing they predate a 315 ka boundary is near-guaranteed by construction: anything with a deep-time first appearance predates a recent boundary. The arithmetic confirms the dates are old; it does not test the inheritance thesis. The paper half-admits this ("the paper measures nothing new"), which is honest but also concedes the simulation is a presentation device, not evidence.
- **Non-independence inflates the count.** §10: "stone-flake production" (3.3), "large-animal butchery" (2.9), "Oldowan technology" (2.6), and "Acheulean biface" (1.76) are not four independent capacities — they are nested points on one lithic/subsistence trajectory. Counting them as four of eight pads both "seven of eight" and the median. This is the strongest single methodological objection.
- **Category error in quantification.** §10 treats an anatomical trait, several technologies, a behavioral pattern (dispersal), and a fire trace as commensurable members of one "set" with a "span" and "median." The quantification lends false precision to what is a heterogeneous list.
- **Selective first-appearances.** §10's "first appearance" of butchery (2.9 Ma) silently passes over older contested claims (Dikika cut-marks ~3.4 Ma; Bouri ~2.5 Ma). The "single securely dated onset" rule is defensible but is doing quiet selection work that the prose presents as settled.
- **Thesis is mainstream, not novel.** The core argument — sapiens inherited rather than invented its capacities — is consensus paleoanthropology. That is fine for an essay; for a Nature-level *contribution* claim, the paper offers synthesis and a veneer of measurement rather than a new finding.
- **Genuinely strong:** the refusal to let "behavioral modernity" do too much work (§8); the insistence that the category be "historical before it can be moral" (§7); the disciplined separation of fire *presence* from fire *mastery* (§5); and the conservatism of dating bipedality to Ardipithecus (4.4 Ma) rather than the contested Sahelanthropus (7 Ma) while still discussing the latter.

## Factual errors
- **Schöningen ~300,000 yr attributed to Thieme 1997** (§6, §10) — Thieme reported ~400,000; current dating ~200,000. Both a citation-support error and a live factual vulnerability. (Verified.)
- **"Detrait/Detroit"** for Détroit (refs/sim). (Verified.)
- **Gona "2.5" vs "2.6 Ma"** inconsistency between reference title and body/sim. (Partly verified.)
- **"midpoint used" → 2.9** for the 3.0–2.6 range, where the midpoint is 2.8 (sim annotation). (Verified by arithmetic.)
- The Denisovan entry pinned to **0.200 Ma** in the sim is essentially arbitrary (the lineage long predates and the cave individuals span ~200–50 ka); the code flags "date approximate," and it doesn't feed the headline, so this is venial.
- No other factual errors found; the bulk of the dated claims (Jebel Irhoud 315 ka, Omo I ≥233 ka, Herto ~160 ka, Dmanisi 1.8 Ma, Kokiselei 1.76 Ma, Wonderwerk ~1 Ma, naledi 335–236 ka, Lomekwi 3.3 Ma) check out.

## Prose and insight
**Alive:** the abstract's closing stratigraphic image ("upright bodies before large brains, manipulative hands before secure species assignment, tools before modern anatomy, population networks before global replacement") is the best sentence in the paper and earns its rhythm. §5's "Fire makes a durable center in a mobile life" and "Maintenance enters the social order" are crisp and true. "Carrying turns landscape into a sequence of delayed uses" (§2) is a real insight compactly put. "Denisovans survive in genes before they survive as a well-described skeleton" (§7) is excellent.

**Dead/weak:** §9 ("Origin myths in the record") restates §1 and §8 without adding; "Species labels are indispensable and dangerous" is a sentence the paper has already earned three times. §11's three closing paragraphs say the same thing ("its inheritance is older") in three registers. The repeated "X before Y" anaphora, powerful once, is run to the edge of mannerism by §10.

**Genuine contribution:** a disciplined, well-sourced synthesis arguing that the sapiens label retroactively annexes a deep hominin field — correct, clearly argued, and unusually well cited. The §10 quantification is the *weakest* part dressed as the most rigorous; the essay would be stronger if §10 were reframed as an honest ordering, not a measurement.

## Top fixes
1. **Fix the Schöningen date and its citation.** Either (a) report it as Thieme's ~400 ka, or (b) use a current value with the right source — and in *both* cases add one sentence acknowledging the 2025 re-dating to ~200 ka. Then state explicitly how robust "seven of eight / all eight" is to that uncertainty. Right now the headline silently depends on the one value that gives the cleanest story.
2. **Stress-test the count.** Add a line in §10 showing what happens to the counts under the plausible range of the contested dates (wooden weapons at 200/300/400 ka). A claim that survives its own sensitivity analysis is far stronger than a brittle "seven of eight."
3. **Address non-independence.** Either collapse the nested lithic capacities (stone-flake / butchery / Oldowan / Acheulean) into fewer items or explicitly defend counting them separately. As written it pads the headline.
4. **Drop or downgrade the "median lead 1.885 Ma."** A median over heterogeneous first-appearance dates is not meaningful; report the ordering and the deepest/shallowest leads, which *are* defensible, and cut the spurious central statistic.
5. **Reconcile the abstract's eight with §10's eight.** Replace "extended social learning" in the abstract enumeration with "Oldowan technology," or footnote that the headline count refers to the §10 set.
6. **Correct "Détroit"** in both the reference list and the simulation; fix the Gona 2.5/2.6 inconsistency; fix the "midpoint used" annotation (2.8, not 2.9).
7. **Trim §9 and §11.** Cut one of the two redundant closings and fold §9's non-novel restatements into §8; the argument is finished before the paper stops.
